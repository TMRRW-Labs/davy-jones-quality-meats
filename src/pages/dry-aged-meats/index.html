FAQ

Questions About Dry-Aged Beef.

What's the difference between dry aged and wet aged?

Wet aging happens in the bag, where the beef ages in its own moisture. Dry aging happens in open air in a controlled cooler, allowing moisture to leave the beef and the flavour to become more concentrated. The process also reduces the weight of the cut, which is part of why dry-aged beef costs more.

Why prime rib and not tenderloin?

Dry aging works especially well with marbled beef, and prime rib has the marbling for the process to work with. Tenderloin is already very tender but much leaner, so there is less for dry aging to bring out.

How far ahead should I call?

For a holiday or special occasion, call as early as you can. Quantities depend on what's available in the locker and can be limited. For a normal weekend, a few days is usually enough.

Can you cut it a specific way?

Absolutely. Tell us how many ribs you need, the thickness of your steaks, or how you'd like the cut prepared. Custom cutting is included at no extra charge.

Can I order ahead?

Yes. In fact, we recommend it for dry-aged beef because quantities are limited. Call the shop, tell us what you're looking for and when you need it, and we'll let you know what's available.
